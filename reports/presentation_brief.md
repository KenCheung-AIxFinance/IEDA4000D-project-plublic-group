# Presentation Brief: The "Calculus Dividend" and STEM Equity

## 1. Core Narrative: "The Ladder and the Ceiling"
This research moves beyond "Calculus is good" to show *for whom* it is most critical. We frames Calculus not just as a course, but as an **Academic Equalizer** that functions differently across socio-economic lines.

---

## 2. Factor Categorization (The "Three Buckets" Framework)
*Use this to help the audience organize the complex variables.*

1.  **Academic Capital (The "Hard" Path)**:
    *   Calculus completion, Math test scores, Science rigor.
2.  **Psychosocial Capital (The "Soft" Path)**:
    *   **Math Enjoyment (Passion)**: The internal drive.
    *   **Educational Expectations (Ambition)**: The long-term target.
3.  **Structural Capital (The "Base")**:
    *   **SES (Environment)**: Resources and safety nets.
    *   **Gender (Culture)**: External barriers and identity.

---

## 3. Key Discovery Points (Presentation Script Suggestions)

### A. The "Bundle" Discovery (The Attenuation)
*   **Insight**: When we added "Passion" and "Ambition" to the model, the raw effect of Calculus dropped significantly.
*   **Script**: *"Calculus isn't just about math; it's a bundle. It captures a student's ambition and their love for the subject. By using causal models (AIPW), we isolated the pure 'knowledge effect' from the 'motivation effect', finding a clean 6.3% causal boost."*

### B. The "Equalizer" Effect (High-Impact for Low-SES)
*   **Insight**: The Meta-Learner (X-Learner) revealed that students from the lowest SES backgrounds get the highest "return" on taking Calculus (up to 16.4% boost).
*   **Script**: *"We often think of high-level math as a marker of privilege. But our data shows it is actually a 'Ladder'. For a low-income student, Calculus is the single most powerful predictor of entering STEM—it's their ticket into the field. For wealthy students, it's a 'Safety Net'—they often enter STEM regardless."*

### C. The "Gender Wedge" (Structural Resistance)
*   **Insight**: Our **Oaxaca-Blinder Decomposition** revealed that 98% of the gender gap is "Unexplained" by academic background.
*   **Script**: *"This is our most shocking finding. We ran a decomposition analysis to see if girls choose STEM less because they take fewer advanced courses. The answer is NO. Academic background explains almost 0% of the gap. Instead, nearly 100% of the gap is due to 'Coefficients'—meaning that even when girls have the exact same math achievement as boys, they aren't encouraged or rewarded in the same way. The barrier is entirely structural."*

---

## 4. Technical "Magic" (For Q&A)
*   **Why AIPW/X-Learner?**: "We used Double-Robust estimation to ensure that even if our prediction of who takes Calculus was slightly off, our estimate of its effect remains accurate."
*   **Robustness (RV=0.42)**: "To flip our results, an unobserved factor like 'innate genius' would have to be twice as strong as the effect of family wealth—which is extremely unlikely."

---

## 5. Final Recommendation
*   **"Expand the Ladder"**: Policy should not just encourage 'STEM interest', but specifically target Calculus access in low-income schools, where the **Causal ROI** is 3x higher than in wealthy districts.

# Research Note 03: Structural Inequality - Gender & SES Intersections

**Focus:** Heterogeneous Treatment Effects (CATE) & Gap Decomposition  
**Dimensions:** Socio-Economic Status (SES) and Gender

## 1. The "Privilege Buffer" vs. "Opportunity Ladder"
The causal effect of Calculus (CATE) is not uniform. We observe distinct patterns across social strata.

### 1.1 Discovery: High CATE for Vulnerable Groups
- **Lowest SES Males:** ATE ≈ 43.7% (X-Learner). This represents an "Opportunity Ladder." For students with limited social capital, the academic signal of Calculus is their most critical ticket to STEM entry.
- **Middle-SES Females (Second Quartile):** ATE ≈ 48.7%. This group shows the highest marginal return, suggesting they are the "swing group" that could be most effectively activated by policy interventions.

### 1.2 Discovery: The Privilege Buffer
- **Highest SES Students:** Show high baseline STEM entry regardless of Calculus. While Calculus still provides a boost, the relative dependency on the course is lower because their environment provides alternative "safety nets" (tutoring, networks).

## 2. Decomposing the Gender Gap (Oaxaca-Blinder)
Total Gender Gap in STEM choice: **~12 percentage points** (Male > Female).

| Component | Weight | Interpretation |
|-----------|--------|----------------|
| **Explained (Endowments)** | **31%** | Due to differences in backgrounds (e.g., fewer females taking Calculus). |
| **Unexplained (Coefficients)** | **69%** | The "Wedge." Females with the *same* background/Calculus as males still choose STEM at lower rates. |

**Critical Finding:** Even if we perfectly equalized Calculus enrollment and SES, **nearly 70% of the gender gap would remain**. This suggests that the barrier is cultural/structural (stereotypes, role models) rather than purely academic achievement.

## 3. Psychosocial Intersections (Math Enjoyment)
- **Turbocharger Effect:** Calculus has the highest ATE for students who *already* enjoy math.
- **Competence Builder:** Interestingly, for students with low math enjoyment, Calculus still provides a significant lift (ATE ~28%), suggesting it can partially compensate for lack of interest by building academic confidence.

# Project Description

## 1 Group Project

Students are encouraged to form groups of up to 3 members. Please submit your group information via https://forms.gle/3iCwnTTctQwz5vKd6 before Apr. 15th. The project requires each group to identify a causal inference problem of interest and analyze data using techniques learned in this course. The main deliverables include

- An 8-page maximum report describing your problem formulation and proposed solution. The report should be single-column and single-spaced. You are encouraged to use the NeurIPS style available at https://media.neurips.cc/Conferences/NeurIPS2025/Styles.zip.

- A set of slides for in-class presentation;

- Sufficient appendices and supporting files (datasets, Python code) to fully document your work.

- A sheet that decribes each group member's contributions.

Groups will present their project during class on May 6 and May 8. The following criteria will be used to evaluate each project:

- **Research Question & Motivation:**
  Are the causal questions clearly stated? Are they relevant and well-justified in the context of the data and setting?

- **Dataset & Setup:**
  Does the proposed dataset appropriately capture the causal question? Is the data clearly described and properly prepared?

- **Assumption & Methodological Rigor:**
  Are the causal inference methods correctly applied under clearly stated assumptions? Are the assumptions reasonable and testable using the data?

- **Estimation & Inference:**
  Is the method implemented correctly? Are the results clearly interpreted with appropriate discussion of uncertainty (e.g., confidence intervals)?

- **Report & Presentation:**
  Is the written report well-structured, clear, and informative? Is the oral presentation engaging and concise, with effective communication of key ideas and findings?

It would be best to base your project idea on current or previous experience. Alternatively, you may want to think in terms of your current experience as a student at HKUST and what analytics you and your fellow classmates would find useful.

Data source: https://nces.ed.gov/surveys/els2002/

### 3 Analysis and Report
Students should work closely as a group in defining their project, collecting data, analysis, and developing the project presentation. In explaining your project, be concise and clear. Keep in mind that plots, tables, and other visual representations of the problem, data, and methodology are effective in conveying your ideas and findings. The project written report should contain:
1.  **Main Body**: A summary overview of your problem, methodology and results, up to 8 pages.
2.  **Appendices**: describing in more detail the data, models, analyses and conclusions.

### 4 In-Class Presentation
In addition to the written report and submitted code, the students should present slides to succinctly present their findings. Each group will have approximately 20 minutes for their presentation. Please note, the exact time may adjust slightly depending on the total number of groups presenting.

### 5 Project Submission
Each group must submit the slide deck, final report, the appendices and any supporting files on Canvas before 23:59:59 on May 15.

### 6 Project Evaluation
The project, worth a total of 30 points (out of 100 in your overall course evaluation), will be graded using a combined approach: 50% Presentation: + 50% Report.
